# Al Brooks — Trading Price Action Reversals (2012) — Comprehensive Notes

## PART I: Trend Reversals — A Trend Becoming an Opposite Trend

### Part I Introduction — Core Principles

**The #1 Rule**: Never trade against a trend until after there has been a breakout of the channel (a break beyond a significant trend line). Even then, take a reversal trade only if there is a strong signal bar.

**Most reversal attempts fail.** A market has inertia — strong propensity to continue, strong resistance to change. Most reversal patterns are trading ranges, and ~80% of trading range breakouts are in the direction of the prior trend.

**Reversal sequence (all time frames)**:
1. Trend is in a channel
2. Move breaks the trend line (breaks out of channel)
3. Market moves back in direction of old trend (test of extreme)
4. If test fails → second leg against old trend → new trend or trading range

**Key distinction**: A reversal TRADE vs. a countertrend SCALP:
- Reversal trade = always-in flip is likely
- Countertrend scalp = NOT a reversal; usually bad trader's equation; forms within channels
- Channels constantly sucker traders into countertrend trades using stop entries — these traders get trapped

**Failed reversals become continuation setups**: Any failed breakout that fails in its attempt to resume the trend becomes a breakout pullback in the new trend.

**Test of old extreme**: Can be higher high, double top, or lower high (at bull top); lower low, double bottom, or higher low (at bear bottom). Overshoots and undershoots both valid.

**Trading range outcomes**: Most trend reversals lead to trading ranges, not opposite trends (~70% of the time). Even so, the reversal entry usually goes far enough for a swing trade.

**Climactic reversals**: More common at bottoms than tops. Big bars, huge volume, several pushes up and down, failed flags, three-push patterns. Tops more often come from trading ranges (double top, H&S) followed by strong bear spike.

**Head and shoulders reality**: ALL H&S tops/bottoms are really continuation patterns (flags). They are trading ranges — much more likely to break out in direction of trend. Only rarely do they reverse the trend.

**Double tops/bottoms reality**: Same — most are continuation patterns. When one DOES reverse, traders apply the reversal pattern name to describe the shape of the range.

**Final flags**: Most climactic reversals are variations of final flag reversals. The bull/bear flag becomes the final flag when the expected breakout fails and reverses.

**The rhythm**: Trends alternate. One trend ends with trend channel line overshoot and reversal → two-legged move breaking trend line. Those two legs form a channel for the new trend.

**Mathematics of reversals**:
- Strong setup: ≥60% chance of minimum two-legged move lasting ≥10 bars
- Profit target usually 2× or more of risk
- Most initial entries: ~40-50% probability of success
- Waiting for clear always-in flip: increases probability to 60%+ but reduces reward
- Average setup: 40% profitable swing, 30% small loss, 30% small profit
- Best setups: 60% profitable swing

**Best reversal setup**: End of a pullback — when short-term countertrend move is ending and reversing back into direction of major trend. i.e., bull flag breaking out upside in bull trend, bear flag reversing back down in bear trend.

**Counter-trend scalp trap** (Low 1/2/3 in bear, High 1/2/3 in bull):
- In bear trend looking for reversal up: Low 1 fails → low 2 fails → possible wedge bear flag
- Must exit if low 2 or 3 triggers in strong bear trend
- ~80% of the time, low 2 or 3 in strong bear trend hits stops below higher low
- This is why low 2/3 shorts are so reliable in strong bear trends, high 2/3 longs in strong bull trends

**Most traders will NOT take any reversal trade unless**: there is at least a double top/bottom, micro double top/bottom, OR a final flag.

### Chapter 2: Signs of Strength in a Reversal

**Strong BULL reversal characteristics:**
1. Strong bull reversal bar — large bull body, small/no tails
2. Next 2-3 bars also have bull bodies ≥ average size
3. Spike grows to 5-10 bars without pulling back more than 1 bar; reverses many bars, swing highs, bear flags
4. Bars in spike: low at or just 1 tick below prior close
5. Bars in spike: open above prior close
6. Bars in spike: close on high or 1 tick below
7. Context makes reversal likely (higher low / lower low test after trend line break)
8. First/second bar closes above highs of many prior bars
9. First pullback only after 3+ bars
10. First pullback lasts only 1-2 bars, not after strong bear reversal bar
11. First pullback does NOT hit breakeven stop
12. Spike breaks several resistance levels (MA, prior swing highs, trend lines) by many ticks
13. As first bar forms, spends most time near high; pullbacks < 1/4 of bar height
14. Sense of urgency — want to buy but pullback never comes
15. Second attempt to reverse (second signal)
16. Reversal began from overshoot of trend channel line
17. Reversing a significant swing high/low
18. High 1 and High 2 pullbacks have strong bull reversal signal bars
19. Trending "anything": closes, highs, lows, or bodies
20. Pullbacks are small and sideways
21. Prior breaks of earlier bear trend lines (not first sign of bullish strength)
22. Test of bear low lacks momentum — overlapping bars, many bull trend bars
23. Test of bear low fails at MA or old bear trend line
24. Breakout reverses many recent closes and highs (close reversal stronger than high reversal)

**Strong BEAR reversal** — exact mirror of above.

**Rule**: The stronger the prior trend, the more signs of strength needed in the reversal for a profitable countertrend trade. Weak trend → can short weaker setups. Top of trading range → may not even need reversal bar.

### Chapter 3: Major Trend Reversal

**Four requirements for major trend reversal:**
1. A trend on the chart
2. Countertrend move strong enough to break trend line (and usually MA)
3. Test of trend's extreme → second reversal (higher high/double top/lower high at top; lower low/double bottom/higher low at bottom)
4. Second reversal goes far enough for consensus that trend has reversed

**Key rules:**
- Trend line break ALONE does not reverse the trend — it's the first significant sign
- After trend line break, still trade WITH trend (expect test of old extreme)
- Take countertrend trade ONLY if reversal setup develops during test
- If break doesn't even reach MA → not strong enough for reversal, trend still strong
- If test goes 5+ bars above old extreme in steep channel → begin process over (wait for new trend line break)

**Test momentum matters:**
- Very tight, steep channel test with no pullbacks → likely trend resumption
- Overlapping bars, large counter-trend bars, 2-3 pullbacks, wedge shape, less steep → likely reversal or trading range

**Higher high vs. lower high (at bull top)**:
- All are variations of double tops — market doesn't care how perfect
- Higher high = start of first leg down; likely followed by lower high second entry
- Lower high = start of second leg down

**Fibonacci**: 61.8% retracement "never as precise as the number leads one to believe" — probably no more likely than 60%, 63%, or 67%

**Average major trend reversal probability:**
- ~40% chance of profitable swing
- ~30% chance of small profit
- ~30% chance of small loss (protective stop hit)
- Best setups: 60%+ chance of profitable swing, only 30-40% chance of stop hit

**Don't look for reversals on smaller time frames**: Until clear reversal on 5-min chart, treat all 1/3-min reversal attempts as with-trend setups (bear flags in bear trend). Enter where trapped countertrend traders will exit.

**Micro double bottoms/tops**: Most tradable reversals begin with at least one of:
- Micro double bottom/top
- Traditional double bottom/top
- Final flag reversal
Most traders will NOT take reversal trade without one of these present.

## Chapter 4: Climactic Reversals

### Core Concept
- A climax = any market that has gone too far, too fast; unsustainable behavior
- Every trend bar is a breakout and a potential climax
- Climactic reversal = climax followed by a spike in the opposite direction (failed breakout)
- On a higher time frame, every climactic reversal is simply a two-bar reversal or a single reversal bar

### Key Rules
1. **Consecutive climaxes**: 2nd or 3rd consecutive buy/sell climax without significant correction → expect ≥10-bar, ≥2-legged correction
2. **Third consecutive climax** usually most dramatic — often has largest trend bar, overshoots trend channel lines, creates parabolic bend
3. **Vacuum effect**: strong bulls/bears step aside waiting for better price → creates vacuum that sucks market to their target → aggressive entry there creates sharp reversal
4. **Spike tests**: Up spikes in bull trends and down spikes in bear trends usually get tested and exceeded. Counter-trend spikes (down in bull, up in bear) are pullbacks and may NOT get tested
5. **V bottoms/tops are rare** — only a few times per month on 5-min charts. Most reversal spikes get tested before trend reversal is confirmed
6. **Volume**: 10-20× average volume on reversal bar → higher odds of follow-through; but volume patterns NOT reliable enough to base trades on alone
7. **Biggest trend bar after protracted trend** often = exhaustive climax, NOT strong breakout. Probability of success <30% for continuation
8. **After buy climax with 10+ bar correction** → excess worked off → next buy climax less likely to reverse dramatically
9. **Trend channel line overshoot + reversal** → usually ≥10-bar, ≥2-legged correction; if re-enters channel, usually pokes through opposite side

### Trading Rules
- **Entry on consecutive sell climaxes**: Buy close of large bear bar, below its low, or above next bar's high. Risk ≈ height of climax bar. ≥60% chance of test of high before stop hit
- **Countertrend entries**: Wait for 2nd entry (lower high for shorts at top, higher low for longs at bottom). Most first entries fail
- **Don't fade first attempt** to break out of tight channel — most fail before going far enough for scalp
- **Parabolic acceleration** (increasing slope) = climactic behavior → imminent correction
- **Big up + big down = confusion = trading range** for a while
- Only experienced traders should short buy climaxes — easy to misread

### Probabilities
- Most breakout attempts fail
- After 3 consecutive climaxes without correction: ~60%+ chance of ≥10-bar, ≥2-legged correction
- V reversals without test: rare, only few times/month on 5-min
- ~50% chance breakout above bull channel followed by reversal will continue below bottom of channel

## Chapter 5: Wedges and Three-Push Reversal Patterns

### Core Concept
- Three pushes in one direction → reversal: first target = start of pattern, next = measured move based on height
- ALL three-push patterns are the same underlying behavior: wedges, micro wedges, parabolic wedges, triple tops/bottoms, H&S, expanding triangles, double top/bottom pullbacks
- Wedge = trend channel with three pushes, often with converging trend line and trend channel line

### Wedge Pullback vs Wedge Reversal
- **Pullback**: with-trend → enter on first signal. Bull flag points DOWN, bear flag points UP
- **Reversal**: countertrend → wait for second entry (higher low or lower low). Bull reversal points DOWN (at bottom), bear reversal points UP (at top)
- Pullbacks usually 10-20 bars; reversals need ≥20 bars and clear trend channel line to be strong enough

### Key Rules
1. **Three pushes + reversal signal**: ≥60% chance of ≥2-legged correction lasting ≥10 bars
2. **Shrinking stairs** (each breakout smaller than prior): waning momentum → increases reversal odds
3. **Failed wedge**: often runs measured move equal to height of wedge in breakout direction
4. **Tight channel wedge**: DON'T enter countertrend on first signal. Wait for breakout and trade breakout pullback
5. **4th push**: If 2nd push is very strong, count may reset — what looks like 4th push is really 3rd
6. **Opening reversals**: steep first-hour wedges CAN be reliable reversal setups, unlike similar patterns later in day
7. **Micro wedge**: 3 consecutive bars with same-direction tails → minor correction only in strong trend
8. **Wedge top in strong bull trend**: often corrects sideways to MA → sets up reliable high 2 buy (NOT a reversal setup)
9. **Successive 1-tick breakouts**: 2 in same direction = three-push pattern → breakout usually gets measured move

### Failed Wedge Indicators
- Market goes sideways instead of reversing after wedge → accepting lower prices, not rejecting them → likely to continue
- No prior trend line break or countertrend momentum → wedge reversal unlikely to work
- Tight channel → don't buy/sell first reversal attempt

## Chapter 6: Expanding Triangles

### Core Concept
- Three or more pushes that alternate direction, each extending beyond the prior push
- Variant: five, seven, or nine pushes, always odd number (last push sets up countertrend entry)
- Trading range variation — most breakouts fail, pulled back into range
- All three-push patterns trade the same regardless of shape (convergent, parallel, or divergent)

### Key Rules
1. **Odd number of pushes** (3, 5, 7, 9) → enter countertrend on the final push
2. Last push often traps traders → forced exit drives the reversal
3. If 5th push succeeds, pattern evolves into trending range; if it fails, a 7th push may develop
4. When trapped in a losing position from the triangle, forced exit drives move in opposite direction

## Chapter 7: Final Flags

### Core Concept
- Every reversal follows some kind of flag → every reversal is a type of final flag reversal
- Final flag = the last flag before a trend reverses into a trading range or opposite trend

### Characteristics of Final Flags
1. Flag occurs in trend that has gone on for dozens of bars — traders begin taking profits
2. Flag is mostly horizontal with strong two-sided trading (opposite trend bars, prominent tails, overlapping bars)
3. Can be as small as a single bar (one-bar final flag, especially after consecutive climaxes)
4. Tight trading ranges often become final flags — magnetic pull draws breakout back
5. Sometimes no breakout — final flag just grows into new opposite trend (common after consecutive climaxes)

### Three Breakout Modes for Final Bull Flag
1. **Breakout goes above old high** → reverses down from higher high
2. **Breakout weaker** → reverses at lower high (below prior high)
3. **No upside breakout at all** → breaks downside directly (bear bar = spike, followed by bear channel)

### Key Rules
1. **Consecutive climaxes + small pause/flag + breakout from flag** → breakout often = another climax → total = consecutive climaxes → expect ≥10-bar correction
2. **Micro final flag**: tail or opposite-body bar at bottom of spike → traders expect one more push + then pullback → that push is the final scalp
3. **Failed final flag** = breakout pullback setup for trend resumption. If final flag reversal triggers but fails within 1-2 bars, the trend resumes
4. **Small final flag evolves into larger final flag**: breakout of small flag reverses briefly, then new larger flag forms. You can usually scalp the first one
5. **1-bar final flag after consecutive climaxes**: pause bar (doji, opposite-body bar) → next bar/two is another climax → then reversal. Traders buy/sell on close of that climax bar
6. **Low 1 / High 1 in flag after reversal**: Expected to fail → fade them (buy low 1 short signal, sell high 1 buy signal) with limit orders
7. **Head and shoulders bottoms**: most become bear flags (continuation), not reversals

## Chapter 8: Double Top and Bottom Pullbacks

### Core Concept
- Double bottom → rally → pullback tests near double bottom = **double bottom pullback** long setup
- Three-push bottom (triple bottom / triangle / H&S bottom) where 3rd push fails to make new low
- The move up from 2nd bottom = breakout; the 3rd push down = breakout pullback — one of the most reliable setups
- Does NOT matter if pattern looks like triple bottom, triangle, H&S, or double bottom pullback — significance identical

### Key Rules
1. Second bottom slightly BELOW first is common and actually preferable
2. If second bottom is ABOVE first → risk it's just a two-legged correction, not reversal → scalp only
3. Both double bottom AND double top in same range → triangle → breakout mode
4. Double top pullback = mirror image: double top → selloff → rally tests near double top high = short setup
5. Double bottom bull flag = continuation pattern (in established trend); double bottom pullback = reversal pattern (beginning of trend)
6. Strong signal bar on 3rd push greatly increases reliability
7. When 3rd push can't even reach the double bottom → bulls are urgent → strong buy

## Chapter 9: Failures

### Core Concept
- A failure = any trade that doesn't reach its goal (smaller profit or loss)
- Every failed pattern creates excellent opposite-direction setup — trapped traders fuel the move
- ~60% success rate → ~40% failure rate for good setups

### Common Failures Leading to Opposite-Direction Trades
1. Failed H1/H2/L1/L2
2. Failed breakout of swing high/low, trend line, trend channel line
3. Failed final flag (becomes breakout pullback for trend resumption)
4. Failed wedge
5. Failed signal/entry bar (protective stops run)
6. Failed one-tick breakout
7. Failed reversal (failed double bottom/top, failed H&S)
8. Failed profit target (5-tick, 9-tick, 17-tick failures in Emini)

### One-Tick Failed Breakout Mechanics
- Market goes 1 tick above prior bar → smart money shorts there with limit orders → overwhelms new longs
- New longs get nervous as market doesn't immediately continue → they exit → adds selling pressure
- Trapped longs become sellers → one-sided market → reliable scalp in opposite direction
- One-tick failures on 5-min often also appear on 15/60-min charts while bar is forming, but get corrected before close

### Profit Target Failures (Emini-specific)
- **5-tick failure**: Market needs 6 ticks beyond signal bar for 4-tick scalp profit. Only reaches 5 → loss of momentum → pullback/reversal imminent
- **9-tick failure**: Trying for 2-point profit needs 10 ticks above signal bar high. Reaches only 9 → never let trade turn into loss → trail stops
- **17-tick failure**: Trying for 4-point profit needs 18 ticks. Reaches 17 → bulls taking profit 1 tick early = lack of confidence → profit-taking cascade
- **If market reaches 16 ticks**: ~80% chance of reaching 17-18 ticks → usually unwise to exit at 16 unless reversal signal

### Stop-Running Mechanics
- Protective stops at 1 tick beyond signal/entry bars → market often runs these exact levels → strong trend bar → scalper's profit in opposite direction
- Breakout tests often test entry price to exact tick → trap weak hands → they chase market at worse price
- If breakout test MISSES stops by 1 tick → forms double bottom bull flag → strong with-trend setup

### Trading Range Breakout Failures
- Strong two-sided trading → breakouts above swing highs and below swing lows usually fail
- Fade new swing highs/lows, especially second entries and when momentum is not too strong
- In trading range: buy low, sell high; most breakout attempts fail no matter how strong they look

## PART II: Day Trading

## Chapter 10: Huge Volume Reversals on Daily Charts

- Stock in steep bear trend + day with 5-10× average volume + strong bull close → tradable bottom likely
- Huge volume WITHOUT bullish price action = no reason to fade the trend
- On 1-min Emini: huge volume bar (~25,000 contracts) in bear trend → NOT end of trend, but pullback coming after 1-2 lower lows on less volume (volume divergence)
- Rarely look at volume on intraday charts — unreliable predictive value on 5-min

## Chapter 11: Key Times of the Day

### Three Periods
1. **First hour (opening range)**: Easiest to make AND lose money. Reversals common/abrupt. Either high or low of day forms here ~90% of time. Best trader's equation (reward 2-3× risk). Focus on swing setups
2. **Middle third (~8:30-11:30 PST)**: Two-sided trading, tight ranges, channels. Worst for stop entries. Institutions buy low/sell high with limits. Beginners should avoid or be very selective. Market often reverses ~8:30 PST
3. **Final third (to close)**: Often resumes earlier trend (trend resumption day) or reverses. Risk managers force losing traders to cover → relentless trends into close. Momentum programs amplify

### Key Rules
- First bar = high or low of day only ~20% of time → no rush to enter on bar 2
- High or low forms within first hour ~90% of days
- Bull trend on daily chart → most days close above open → expect rally into close
- Reports at 7:00 PST: computers have speed edge → don't compete → wait for their edge to disappear
- Three periods often create up-down-up or down-up-down trend resumption pattern

## Chapter 12: Markets
- Trade stocks popular with institutions (reduce manipulation risk, minimal slippage)
- Emini can handle any individual trader's order size
- Consider SPY instead of Emini when starting out (1 Emini ≈ 500 SPY)
- Focus on increasing position size over adding lower-probability entries

## Chapter 13: Time Frames and Chart Types

- **5-min candle chart**: best for most traders — enough setups, readable speed, precise trend lines
- 3-min: more trades, smaller stops, but lower winning percentage
- 1-min: useful only in two situations: (1) finding H1/H2 entries in runaway 5-min trends, (2) entering at 5-min MA touches in trending stocks
- 90-bar EMA on 1-min ≈ 20-bar EMA on 5-min (NOT 100-bar, due to recalculation weighting)
- Price action works identically on ALL chart types (time, volume, tick) — human behavior is genetic
- Pick ONE chart and stick with it — multiple charts cause missed trades and confusion

## Chapter 14: Globex, Premarket, Postmarket
- Same price action techniques apply 24 hours
- Premarket extremes often tested during day session
- Day session chart alone provides adequate signals
- Post-earnings moves usually too fast/volatile for most traders
- Moving average can differ between Globex and day session for first hour — use momentum, not MA

## Chapter 15: Always In

### Core Concept — Most Important in Trading
- If forced to be in market at all times (long or short), which direction? That's the always-in position
- Determines trend direction; guides all trade selection
- If signal clear enough for always-in reversal → ≥60% chance of reaching profit target ≥ risk

### Determining Always-In Direction
- Two consecutive reasonably strong trend bars in same direction → probable always-in direction
- Single strong trend bar can flip it if context is right
- Follow-through bar is critical: bear close after bear breakout confirms; bull close negates
- If uncertain → market is in trading range → buy low, sell high, scalp

### Key Rules
1. **Don't take countertrend trades** when always-in direction is clear — almost certainly scalps with risk ≥ reward needing unrealistic 70%+ win rate
2. **80% of reversal attempts fail** in trends → each reversal = buying/selling opportunity for with-trend traders
3. Strong bull spikes where traders feel urgency = perfect always-in long; desperate for pullback that never comes
4. Most traders: look for 3-10 best swings/day, not every minor reversal
5. In trading range: always-in is dangerous — buy low/sell high instead
6. Sell vacuum at bottom of trading range day creates strong bear spike that looks like breakout → usually fails → fade it
7. Once always-in is established, enter at least small position at market — don't wait for pullback that may never come
8. Scale into positions in direction of trend using fixed intervals based on average daily range

## Chapter 16: Extreme Scalping
- ~40+ scalp opportunities per day on 5-min Emini (stop and limit entries combined)
- Virtually impossible for most traders to execute profitably in real time
- Both bulls and bears can scalp at same price if they manage trades correctly
- Demonstrates how much subtle price action information exists on every chart
- Most traders should NOT attempt — focus on 2-4 point swings instead

## PART III: The First Hour (Opening Range)

### Opening Range Characteristics
- "First hour" = time before first good swing begins (can be 15 min to 2 hours)
- Easiest time to make money AND lose money
- Yesterday's always-in direction often carries into today's open
- Reversals: common, abrupt, large — but hard to process fast enough
- First bar = high or low of day only ~20% of time
- High of day usually begins with some type of double top; low with double bottom
- ~90% of days: high or low forms within first 90 minutes
- Opening range size predicts day type:
  - ~25% of average range → breakout mode → possible trend from open
  - ~33-50% of average range → will likely expand to average → trending trading range day (~2/3) or trading range day (~1/3)
  - Large opening move → spike and channel trend day or climactic reversal

### Key Trading Rules
1. Beginners should NOT scalp in first hour — wait for always-in swing setup
2. Most reversals have only ~30% chance of 2× reward swing → scalp for ≥1× risk instead (60%+ probability)
3. Wait for double top/bottom before looking for swing → ~40-50% chance of swing
4. Strong trend bar for signal bar → must take the trade
5. Don't confuse swing and scalp setups — different trader's equations
6. If in doubt, reduce position size and use wider stop

## Chapter 17: Patterns Related to Premarket
- Globex highs/lows often tested during day session
- Most trades on Globex chart also have day session price action reasons for same entry
- Moving average can be opposite direction on Globex vs day session for first 60-90 minutes
- Choose either Globex OR day session chart — not both

## Chapter 18: Patterns Related to Yesterday
- Many first-hour trades relate to prior day patterns
- Look at final 1-2 hours of yesterday for: strong trends, trading ranges, channels, patterns
- Bull channel from yesterday = bear flag → often breaks below on open
- Bear channel from yesterday = bull flag → breakout above on open common
- Evaluate every open: breakout → possible reversal (failed breakout) → if reversal fails → breakout pullback

## Chapter 19: Opening Patterns and Reversals
- Institutions have orders to fill → buy/sell vacuums create sharp opening reversals
- These sharp reversals often become high or low of day
- Double top bear flag in first hour: common setup, often followed by protracted bear trend
- First bar: strong trend bar = 30% chance of being extreme; doji = trading range day likely
- Two consecutive trend bars in first hour → traders see as possible always-in direction
- ~25% of first-hour reversals result in swings → scalp until double top/bottom or clear always-in

## Chapter 20: Gap Openings
- Gap = breakout = spike → treat as any breakout
- Larger gap → more likely trend day; gap = spike on higher time frame
- Large gap up: ~50% bull channel, ~20% trading range, ~30% bear trend (guidelines only)
- If gap is largest of past 5 days or > half average daily range → large gap
- Swing aggressively on gap days; add to position on pullbacks in direction of trend
- Gap spike and channel: gap is the spike → channel follows in same direction

## Chapter 24: The Best Trades — Putting It All Together

### The Market Is Simple
- Market is one-dimensional (price) — always searching for agreement (trading range) or disagreement (breakout/trend)
- Every breakout attempt met by opposite belief → most fail
- Edge is always small and fleeting — but edges appear constantly

### Best Trade Categories (Stop Entries for Beginners)

**High-probability swings (reward ≥ 2× risk, probability 50-60%)**:
1. Opening reversals with strong setup
2. Major trend reversals (after strong trend line break + weak test of extreme + strong reversal bar)
3. Strong final flag reversals
4. Fading 3rd/4th push in stairs patterns
5. Channel reaching measured move target + weakening

**High-probability scalps (reward ≈ risk, probability ≥ 60%)**:
6. H2 pullback to MA in bull trend
7. L2 pullback to MA in bear trend
8. Wedge bull/bear flag pullbacks in trends
9. Breakout pullbacks from flag breakouts
10. H1 in strong bull spike (not after buy climax) / L1 in strong bear spike
11. Second entries at trading range extremes

### Best Trade Categories (Limit Entries for Experienced Traders)
- Buy/sell during strong spikes at market or at/below prior bar
- Fade weak H1/L1 signal bars at range extremes
- Buy/sell quiet flags at MA
- Buy below bull flag breakout bar anticipating breakout pullback
- Buy/sell breakout tests (stop-running attempts)
- Fixed-interval pullback entries in strong trends

### Guidelines for Beginners
1. Reward ≥ risk (ideally 2× risk when starting)
2. Enter only on stops
3. Always have protective stop in market + profit-taking limit order
4. Buy only above bull bars, short only below bear bars
5. Trade small ("I don't care" mode)
6. 3-5 trades/day maximum
7. Focus: opening range trends, strong reversals, trend pullbacks
8. NEVER trade in tight trading ranges
9. NEVER take countertrend scalps until consistently profitable

### Critical Math
- 60% win rate with reward = risk → profitable
- Scalping for reward < risk requires 80%+ win rate → virtually impossible to sustain
- Every trade: probability × reward must be > probability of failure × risk
- If setup looks good → assume ≥60% probability; if uncertain → assume 50% but need reward ≥ 2× risk

### Single Most Reliable Day Trades
- Two-legged pullback to MA in strong trend with bull/bear signal bar
- Enter → scalp half at 1× risk → trail stop on remainder → add at every new with-trend setup
- On strong trend days (~20% of time): every pullback to MA is high-probability entry

## Chapter 25: Trading Guidelines (78 Rules)

### Most Critical Guidelines

1. **Edge is always small** — smart people on both sides of every trade
2. **Every bar is a signal bar for both directions** — be open to all possibilities
3. **When positive your setup is good, don't take the trade** — you're missing something; be humble
4. **Talk yourself out of bad trades** — single most important thing you can do all day
5. **Inertia**: 80% of reversal attempts in trends fail → become flags; 80% of trading range breakout attempts fail
6. **If twisted inside about a pullback going too far** → you're likely seeing a pullback when the trend has reversed
7. **Price is truth** — never argue with the market; fundamentals useless for day trading
8. **Good fill, bad trade** — be suspicious when market gives you better price than expected
9. **"I don't care!"** — most useful mantra; trade small enough that losses don't cloud judgment
10. **Never cherry-pick** — good trades catch you by surprise; you're left with the bad ones
11. **Two legs** — always look for two legs; two failed attempts in one direction → opposite direction
12. **Tight trading range trumps everything** — never enter on stops in tight range
13. **Simple is better** — one chart, no indicators, best setups only
14. **Discipline** — most important characteristic of winning traders
15. **Ability to do nothing for hours** — second most important trait
16. **Increase position size** rather than number of setups — 25 Emini contracts × 2 pts/day = 7 figures/year
17. **Will not make money until**: stop countertrend scalps, start trading with-trend pullbacks, wait for trend line break before reversals
18. **Work on it**: print 5-min chart daily, write every setup, do this for years
