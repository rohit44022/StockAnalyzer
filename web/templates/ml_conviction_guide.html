What Is ML Conviction?

Overview

ML Conviction is a machine learning score from 0 to 100 that measures how confident the system is that a trade will succeed. Think of it as a second opinion from a model trained on 30 years of stock market data.

A score of 75% means the model sees patterns that historically led to winning trades 60-84% of the time. A score of 25% means the patterns look more like losing trades.

💡
Think of it like a weather forecast: The model looks at 46 different measurements (like temperature, humidity, wind for weather) and combines them into one confidence number. Just like a 90% chance of rain doesn't guarantee rain, a 90% conviction doesn't guarantee profit — but it significantly improves your odds.
✓
ML Conviction is not a standalone signal. It works alongside the existing system (BB Squeeze methods, safety overrides, position context) to make better hold/sell decisions.

Why It Matters

Overview

The biggest problem for swing traders is conviction whiplash — signals flip daily (HOLD → SELL → BUY → HOLD) and you end up exiting good trades too early or holding bad ones too long.

ML Conviction solves this by answering: "Should I trust the current exit signal, or is it likely noise?"

High Conviction (70-100%)
The model sees strong patterns for success. Even if today's signal says SELL, the system will dampen it and suggest holding — the trade thesis likely still works.
Low Conviction (0-30%)
The model agrees the trade looks weak. If the system was going to dampen a sell signal (because the position is young), it will override that dampening and let the SELL through.

Pipeline — Step by Step

Pipeline

When you click Analyze on a portfolio position, the conviction score is computed in real-time through this pipeline:

Step 1 — Raw Indicators
The system computes 40+ technical indicators from the stock's price/volume data: Bollinger Bands, RSI, MFI, CMF, ATR, VWMACD, Chaikin, and more. These are the same indicators used across the entire application.
Step 2 — Feature Extraction
46 features are extracted from the raw indicators. These fall into 6 groups: Price Position, Momentum, Volume & Flow, Volatility, Sector-Relative, and Cross-Features. Includes micro lookbacks (1-3 day), standard (5-20 day), and macro lookbacks (60-120 day) for multi-timeframe analysis.
Step 3 — Ensemble Prediction
Two models work together: a LightGBM regressor predicting expected return (r-multiple) and a Logistic Regression predicting win probability. Their outputs are blended (70/30 weight) for a more robust signal than either alone. Trained on 400,000+ historical samples across 2,800+ NSE stocks.
Step 4 — Calibration & Confidence Interval
The blended prediction is passed through isotonic calibration to align scores with actual win rates. Two additional quantile models (10th/90th percentile) provide a confidence interval — showing the range of uncertainty around the score.
Step 5 — Score & Drivers
The calibrated probability is scaled to 0-100. The system also identifies which features are pushing the score up (bullish) or down (bearish), giving you a transparent breakdown of why the model scored the way it did.
Raw Indicators → 46 Features → LightGBM + LogisticRegression Ensemble → Isotonic Calibration → Score 0-100 + Drivers + Confidence Interval

What Is Calibration?

Pipeline

Most ML models are overconfident or underconfident in their raw probability outputs. A model might say "80% chance of winning" but when you check, only 60% of those trades actually win.

Isotonic calibration fixes this by learning a mapping from raw probabilities to actual win rates using a separate dataset the model didn't train on. After calibration, when the model says 70%, you can trust that historically, about 70% of trades at that score did win.

💡
Like calibrating a thermometer: If your thermometer always reads 2° too high, calibration learns that correction and applies it automatically. The ML model's "thermometer" is its probability output, and calibration ensures it reads true.

Understanding Drivers

Interpret

Below the score, you'll see a drivers breakdown showing which features are pushing the score up or down. This makes the ML score transparent — you can see why the model is confident or not.

How Drivers Are Computed
For each of the 46 features, the system computes:
Weight = Feature Importance × Deviation from Typical

Feature Importance comes from the trained model — how much predictive power each feature has (learned from 407K training samples).
Deviation from Typical is how far the current value is from the median (typical) value across all stocks. A Volume Ratio of 3.5x when the median is 1.0x is highly unusual and gets a big weight.

The top 4 drivers (by combined weight) are shown, split into bullish (supporting conviction) and bearish (weighing against).
💡
Like a doctor explaining a diagnosis: Instead of just saying "your risk is high", a good doctor says "your cholesterol is elevated and your blood pressure is above normal — those two together drive the risk assessment." The drivers breakdown does the same for your trade's ML conviction.

Dampening Rules

Decisions

ML Conviction integrates with the position-aware dampening system that prevents noisy signal flips from destroying swing trade conviction.

Without ML Conviction (existing rules)
FRESH positions (Day 0-3): Soft SELL signals are dampened to HOLD. Give the trade time to work.
EARLY positions (Day 4-7): Same, unless momentum is STRONG BEARISH.
ACTIVE positions (Day 8-60): No dampening. Signals pass through as-is.
MATURE positions (Day 61+): No dampening. Signals pass through.
With ML Conviction (new rules)
High conviction (≥70%) extends protection: Even in the ACTIVE phase (Day 8-60), if a soft SELL signal fires but the model has 70%+ conviction, the system dampens the SELL to HOLD. The model has strong evidence the trade thesis is still valid.

Low conviction (<30%) overrides protection: Even if a position is FRESH or EARLY (normally dampened), if ML conviction is below 30%, the dampening is removed and the SELL signal passes through. The model agrees the trade looks bad — don't hold on just because it's young.
✓
Net effect: Good trades are held longer (ML extends conviction). Bad trades are exited faster (ML cuts through dampening). The system becomes smarter about which positions deserve patience.

Safety Overrides Are Sacred

Decisions
Critical Rule
ML Conviction never overrides safety overrides (R1-R5). If the safety system says SELL due to stop-loss breach, extreme drawdown, or confirmed breakdown, that SELL stands regardless of ML conviction. Even a 99% conviction score cannot override a safety exit.

This is by design: safety exits protect capital. The ML model is optimized for win-rate prediction, not risk management. A stock can have "winning patterns" and still hit your stop-loss.

Why an Ensemble?
v2 uses two complementary models: LightGBM regressor predicts expected return (r-multiple), while Logistic Regression predicts win probability. Blending them (70/30) combines LightGBM's ability to capture nonlinear patterns with LR's smoothness and stability. The ensemble outperforms either model alone.

Temporal Split — Why It Matters
The model is trained on older data and tested on newer data it has never seen. This is critical because financial markets evolve over time. A model that only works on shuffled data (mixing future and past) would give inflated accuracy that doesn't hold in real trading. Our temporal split ensures the test results reflect realistic forward-looking performance.

Limitations & Caveats

Important
Past ≠ Future
The model learned patterns from 30 years of NSE data (1994-2026). Markets change. A pattern that predicted winning trades in the past may not work in the future. The model is retrained periodically with latest data and real trade feedback to capture evolving market dynamics.
Not Standalone
ML Conviction is one input among many. It works best combined with the BB Squeeze signals, safety overrides, and position-aware dampening. Never use it as the sole decision-making tool.
Liquidity Matters
The model works best on liquid, frequently-traded stocks (large-cap and mid-cap). For thinly-traded small-caps, the indicators that feed the model may be noisy, and the score less reliable.
Black Swan Events
The model cannot predict sudden market crashes, regulatory changes, or company-specific shocks (fraud, delistings). Safety overrides (R1-R5) exist specifically to handle these scenarios — which is why ML Conviction never overrides them.
✓
Bottom line: ML Conviction is a powerful probabilistic tool that meaningfully improves decision quality when used as part of the complete system. It tells you which positions deserve patience and which don't — but it's not a crystal ball.
