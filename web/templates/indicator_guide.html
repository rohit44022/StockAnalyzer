Bollinger Bands Foundation

Bollinger Bands Overlay
Plain English
Imagine drawing a rubber band around a stock's price. When the stock is calm and barely moving, the rubber band gets tight and narrow. When the stock gets wild and volatile, the band stretches wide. The three lines you see are: the middle line (20-day average price), the upper band (average + 2 standard deviations), and the lower band (average - 2 standard deviations). About 95% of all price action happens inside these bands — so when price breaks outside, something significant is happening.
✅ Takeaway: Bollinger Bands are the backbone of the entire system. Every method (M1-M4) is built on where price sits relative to these bands. Think of them as the "playing field" — all the action happens in reference to these lines.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period (Length)20How many days to average — 20 is roughly 1 trading month
Standard Deviation2.0How wide the bands are — 2.0 captures ~95% of price movement
MA TypeSMASimple Moving Average — equal weight to each day
Upper Band = SMA(20) + 2.0 × StdDev(20)
Middle Band = SMA(20)
Lower Band = SMA(20) - 2.0 × StdDev(20)

How each method uses Bollinger Bands:

  • M1 Squeeze Bands get extremely narrow (low volatility) → then price breaks above the upper band = buy signal. The squeeze is like a coiled spring — the tighter it gets, the bigger the breakout.
  • M2 Trend Measures where price sits inside the bands using %b. If it's near the top = strong uptrend.
  • M3 Reversal Looks for "W" shapes (double bottom) and "M" shapes (double top) relative to the bands — classic reversal patterns.
  • M4 Walking Price hugging the upper band = very strong trend that's "walking" along the top. Best trends do this.
💡 Real-world analogy: Think of Bollinger Bands like the lanes on a highway. The middle line is the center lane. Most cars (price) stay within the lanes. When a car suddenly swerves outside the lane, it's either about to crash (reversal) or is an ambulance on an emergency (strong breakout). Your job is to figure out which one it is — the other indicators help with that.
TradingView Note Uses population standard deviation (ddof=0), matching Bollinger's original specification. Some platforms default to sample std (ddof=1) — verify your TradingView setting matches.

Parabolic SAR Exit Signal

Parabolic SAR Overlay
Plain English
SAR stands for "Stop And Reverse". You'll see tiny dots on the chart — either below the candles (bullish, trend is up) or above the candles (bearish, trend has flipped down). When the dots switch from below to above, it's like a traffic light turning red — the trend may be over, and it's time to think about getting out.
🚨 Takeaway: SAR is your primary sell alarm. When dots flip from below price to above price = the system awards +25 exit points. That's the single biggest exit trigger in the entire scoring system. Pay attention to it.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Start (Initial AF)0.02How quickly the dots start chasing price — 0.02 = conservative start
Increment (Step AF)0.02How much faster dots accelerate each bar — stays modest
Maximum AF0.20Speed cap — dots never chase faster than this
● Dots Below Price = Bullish
Trend is up. Hold your position. The dot is your trailing stop level — if price drops to the dot, consider selling.
● Dots Above Price = Bearish
Trend has flipped. This is a major exit signal. The system scores this as +25 exit points — nearly half the threshold to trigger a full exit.
💡 Real-world analogy: SAR dots are like a loyal dog on a leash. When the trend is up, the dog walks behind you (dots below). The leash gets shorter over time as the dog speeds up. When the dog suddenly jumps in front of you (dots above), it's pulling you the other way — time to reverse.

Percent B (%b) Most Important

Bollinger Bands %B Oscillator
⚠ This is the #1 indicator in the system
%b tells you exactly where the current price is relative to the Bollinger Bands, expressed as a number between 0 and 1. %b = 1.0 means price is at the upper band. %b = 0.0 means price is at the lower band. %b = 0.50 means price is right at the middle. Think of it as a thermometer that tells you how "hot" (near the top) or "cold" (near the bottom) a stock is within its normal range. Every single method in the system — M1, M2, M3, M4 — relies on %b for both entry and exit decisions.
⭐ Takeaway: If you could only look at ONE indicator, make it %b. It's the decision-maker. Everything else is confirmation. Mark the three horizontal lines (0.80, 0.50, 0.20) on your TradingView chart and you'll see the system's logic come alive.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period20Same as BB period — derived from the bands themselves
StdDev2.0Same as BB std dev — keeps everything in sync
%b = (Close - Lower Band) / (Upper Band - Lower Band)

Key levels — draw these horizontal lines on TradingView:

0.80
BUY ZONE
Price near the top of the bands. In a strong uptrend, this confirms momentum. M2 triggers a buy here when MFI also confirms.
0.50
MIDLINE
Price at the 20-day average. Neutral territory. In M1, dropping below here from above = exit signal. In M4, this is the "dip-buy" zone.
0.20
SELL ZONE
Price near the bottom. In M2, this triggers a sell when MFI also confirms weakness. Stock is under pressure.

How each method uses %b:

MethodBuy Signal 🟢Sell Signal 🔴
M1 Squeeze Breakout %b crosses above 0.80 after the bands were squeezed tight. The squeeze is the setup; %b > 0.80 is the trigger. %b drops below 0.50 — momentum has faded back to average.
M2 Trend Following %b > 0.80 AND MFI > 80 — price is strong AND money is flowing in. Double confirmation. %b < 0.20 AND MFI < 20 — price is weak AND money is flowing out.
M3 Reversals W-bottom pattern: 1st dip hits %b ≤ 0.05 (very oversold), rallies, 2nd dip stays above %b = 0.20 (showing strength). The 2nd dip being higher means sellers are exhausted. M-top pattern: 1st peak hits %b ≥ 0.95, dips, 2nd peak only reaches %b < 0.80 (weakening). Sellers taking over.
M4 Walking Bands %b stays ≥ 0.85 with 3+ touches of upper band in 10 bars. Price is "walking" along the top. Dip-buy at 0.50-0.65 — pullback to the middle = buying opportunity. %b falls below 0.15 — the walk is over, trend has broken down.
💡 Real-world analogy: %b is like the floor number in an elevator. Ground floor (0.0) = stock is at the bottom of its range. Top floor (1.0) = stock is at the top. You want to buy when the elevator is heading up past the 8th floor (0.80) with momentum — and sell when it's heading down past the 2nd floor (0.20) with no brakes.

Bandwidth (BBW) Squeeze Detector

Bollinger BandWidth Oscillator
Plain English
Bandwidth measures how wide or narrow the Bollinger Bands are. When the bands get really tight (low BBW), it means the stock has been calm and quiet — like a coiled spring. This quietness doesn't last forever. Eventually the stock will make a big move — either up or down. This tight condition is called "The Squeeze" and it's the foundation of Method I (M1). When BBW is at its lowest point in 6 months, a breakout is imminent.
🎯 Takeaway: Low BBW = calm before the storm. The system watches for the 6-month low in BBW (the squeeze) and then waits for the breakout direction. It's the setup that makes M1 trades possible. You don't trade the squeeze itself — you trade the breakout from it.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period20Same as BB — keeps it synchronized
StdDev2.0Same as BB
Rolling Min Lookback126 bars~6 months of daily bars. If BBW hits this low, it's a rare event.
Absolute Squeeze Trigger0.08 (= 8.0 on TV)If BBW drops below this, it's a squeeze regardless of history
Dynamic Tolerance1.05 (5% above)BBW within 5% of the 6-month low also counts as a squeeze
BBW = (Upper Band - Lower Band) / Middle Band

Squeeze = BBW ≤ rolling_min(126) × 1.05 OR BBW ≤ 0.08
💤 Low BBW = Squeeze (Setup Phase)
Bands are tight. Stock is coiling. Don't trade yet — just watch and wait for the breakout. This is the "get ready" signal.
🚀 BBW Rising = Expansion (Action Phase)
Bands are widening. The breakout has begun! If price breaks upward (above upper BB), that's the M1 buy signal. This is the "go" signal.
TradingView Tip TradingView displays BBW as a percentage (multiplied by 100). So the absolute trigger of 0.08 appears as 8.0 on the chart. Add a horizontal line at 8.0 — anything below this line = squeeze territory.
See also Keltner Channels — BBW tells you "bands are narrow." The KC Squeeze tells you "bands are narrow relative to what the stock's range suggests." Together they form the T7 strategy, which backtested to 53% WR / 1.72 PF — a meaningful edge over BBW squeeze alone.
💡 Real-world analogy: BBW is like watching a pressure cooker. As the pressure builds (volatility compresses), the gauge drops lower and lower. At some point, the steam has to escape. The lower the BBW, the more pressure has built up, and the bigger the eventual move. The 6-month low is your "this cooker is about to blow" signal.

RSI (Relative Strength Index) Exit Guard

RSI Oscillator
Plain English
RSI measures how fast and how much a stock's price has gone up recently versus how much it's gone down. It gives you a number from 0 to 100. A high number (above 75) means the stock has been rising too fast — it's "overbought" and could be due for a pullback. A low number (below 30) means it's been falling too fast — it's "oversold." In our system, RSI is primarily an exit tool, not an entry tool — it tells you when to get out, not when to get in.
🚨 Takeaway: RSI above 75 = the stock has run too hot, a cooldown is likely. This is NOT a sell-everything signal by itself, but it adds +25 points to the exit score. Combined with other warnings, it can push you toward selling. Keep it in the "exit radar" category.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period14Standard 14-day lookback — the most widely used setting worldwide
SmoothingWilder's (RMA)A special smoothing method that's standard for RSI. TradingView uses this by default.
RSI = 100 - (100 / (1 + RS))
RS = Average Gain over 14 days / Average Loss over 14 days

Key levels — draw these on TradingView:

75
OVERBOUGHT
Stock has risen too fast. Not sustainable. Adds +25 exit points. Watch for pullback.
45 – 70
HEALTHY ZONE
Sweet spot. Stock is trending up but not overheated. No action needed — let your winners run.
Below 45
WEAKENING
Momentum is fading. If RSI is also declining (slope < -1.0), that adds another +15 exit points.
💡 Real-world analogy: RSI is like a runner's heart rate. A sprinter can hit a very high heart rate (RSI > 75), but they can't sustain it — they'll have to slow down. A marathon runner with a steady heart rate (RSI 45-70) can keep going for miles. You want your stocks to be marathon runners, not sprinters who collapse.

MFI (Money Flow Index) Confirmation

Money Flow Index Oscillator
Plain English
MFI is like RSI's smarter cousin — it does the same thing (measures overbought/oversold) but also considers how much money (volume) is flowing in or out. A high MFI (above 80) means lots of money is aggressively buying the stock. A low MFI (below 20) means lots of money is aggressively selling. It answers the question: "Is real money backing this move, or is it just price floating around?"
💰 Takeaway: MFI is the confirmation buddy for M2 trades. %b says "the stock is strong" and MFI says "yes, real money agrees." Without MFI confirming, you might be fooled by thin-volume moves that fizzle out. Period is set to 10 (half of BB period 20), per Bollinger's own recommendation.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period10Half the BB period (20/2). Bollinger recommends this pairing for best sensitivity.
Typical Price = (High + Low + Close) / 3
Money Flow = Typical Price × Volume
MFI = 100 - (100 / (1 + Positive Money Flow / Negative Money Flow))
Above 80
BIG MONEY BUYING
Aggressive buying with heavy volume. When paired with %b > 0.80 = M2 buy signal.
50
BALANCED
Equal buying and selling. Neutral. In M1, MFI > 50 adds +15 to the entry score.
Below 20
BIG MONEY SELLING
Aggressive selling with heavy volume. When paired with %b < 0.20 = M2 sell signal.

Special use — MFI Divergence (M3):

In M3 reversal patterns, the system looks for MFI divergence: the stock price makes a new low, but MFI makes a higher low (doesn't drop as much). This means even though the price looks weak, the selling pressure is actually drying up — big money has stopped dumping. This is a bullish sign that a reversal (bounce) is coming. The divergence threshold is 5 MFI points (second low must be at least 5 points higher than the first).

CMF (Chaikin Money Flow) Confirmation

Chaikin Money Flow Oscillator
Plain English
CMF tells you whether institutional investors (the "big fish") are accumulating (quietly buying) or distributing (quietly selling) a stock over the past 20 days. It looks at where the stock closes within each day's range — if it consistently closes near the top of the day's range on high volume, big money is buying. Near the bottom on high volume = big money is selling. CMF oscillates between -1 and +1, but most of the time stays between -0.25 and +0.25.
🕵 Takeaway: CMF is your institutional radar. Positive CMF = big players are buying. Negative = they're selling. In M1 squeeze trades, CMF > 0 is one of the 5 required entry conditions. Think of it as asking: "Are the smart money investors on my side?"
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period20Same as BB period — captures one full month of money flow behavior
CLV = ((Close - Low) - (High - Close)) / (High - Low)
CMF = SUM(CLV × Volume, 20) / SUM(Volume, 20)
Above +0.10
Strong Accumulation
Big money is aggressively buying. Adds +5 bonus to M1 entry score. Very bullish.
Above 0
Mild Accumulation
Net buying. CMF > 0 is M1 buy condition #4 (adds +15 to entry score).
Below -0.10
Strong Distribution
Big money is aggressively selling. Avoid buying. Adds bearish weight to all methods.
💡 Real-world analogy: Think of CMF like watching the kitchen exit at a party. If lots of people are coming in through the front door with full plates (closing near highs on high volume) = accumulation. If people are sneaking out the back with empty plates (closing near lows on high volume) = distribution. The party might look fun, but the smart people are already leaving.
T7 Role CMF > 0 is one of the three gates in the T7 Keltner Channel Squeeze strategy. A deep KC squeeze without positive CMF has higher head-fake risk — the compression is real, but no institutional money is backing the expected breakout direction.

ADX (Average Directional Index) Method Picker

Average Directional Index Oscillator
Plain English
ADX answers one simple question: "Is this stock trending, or is it going sideways?" It does NOT tell you whether the trend is up or down — only how strong it is. A high ADX means the stock is making a strong, directional move (great for M1/M2/M4). A low ADX means the stock is drifting sideways with no clear direction (that's where M3 reversals work better).
🧭 Takeaway: ADX helps you pick the right method. If ADX says "trending" — use trend-following methods (M1, M2, M4). If ADX says "no trend" — use reversal methods (M3). Using the wrong method in the wrong regime is one of the most common mistakes.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Period14Standard 14-day smoothing. Most widely used setting.
ADX ≥ 25 (with rising slope)
TRENDING REGIME
Strong directional move in progress. Use M1 M2 M4. Trend-following strategies work best here.
ADX 20 – 25
TRANSITION ZONE
Could go either way. Watch if ADX is rising (trend building) or falling (trend dying). Be cautious.
ADX < 20
MEAN-REVERTING REGIME
Stock is going sideways, no clear trend. Use M3 reversal patterns (W-bottoms, M-tops). Trend methods will get chopped up here.
💡 Real-world analogy: ADX is like a wind speed gauge. It doesn't tell you which direction the wind blows (up or down) — just how strong it is. High wind = set your sails (trend follow). No wind = paddle back and forth in the channel (mean revert). Trying to sail in calm winds is frustrating; trying to paddle in a storm is dangerous.

VWMACD (Volume-Weighted MACD) Direction Lean

MACD Oscillator
Plain English
MACD compares a fast-moving average (12-day) with a slow-moving average (26-day). When the fast one is above the slow one, the stock has recently been rising — bullish. When the fast one is below — bearish. The "histogram" (bar chart) shows how far apart they are. Growing bars = momentum building. Shrinking bars = momentum fading. In our system, MACD provides a directional lean — it adds bullish or bearish weight to the overall score but is not a primary buy/sell trigger by itself.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Fast Period12Short-term momentum (roughly 2.5 weeks)
Slow Period26Longer-term trend (roughly 5.5 weeks)
Signal Period9Smoothing for the signal line — triggers crossovers
MACD Line = EMA(12) - EMA(26)
Signal Line = EMA(9) of MACD Line
Histogram = MACD Line - Signal Line
Bullish lean
MACD line above signal line + positive histogram = upward momentum. Adds bullish weight to the composite recommendation.
Bearish lean
MACD line below signal line + negative histogram = downward momentum. Adds bearish weight. Not enough to trigger a sell alone.
TradingView Tip On TradingView, search for "MACD" (the built-in indicator). Set Fast Length = 12, Slow Length = 26, Signal = 9. The system uses volume-weighted prices internally, but standard MACD with these params is the closest visual equivalent.

ATR (Average True Range) Stop Loss Engine

Average True Range Volatility
Plain English
ATR tells you how much a stock typically moves in a single day. If a stock's ATR is ₹15, that means on an average day it moves about ₹15 between its high and low. This is incredibly useful for setting stop losses — you don't want your stop so tight that normal daily noise triggers it, but not so wide that you lose too much if you're wrong. ATR gives you a scientific way to set stops based on the stock's actual personality.
🛡 Takeaway: ATR is the engine behind all stop-loss calculations in the system. It adapts to each stock's volatility — a volatile stock gets a wider stop, a calm stock gets a tighter one. The system uses three different ATR periods for different purposes: ATR(14) for general trailing stops, ATR(22) for the Chandelier Exit, and ATR(10) for SuperTrend-style stops.
ATR PeriodValueWhat it's used for
Primary14Trailing stops (tight and wide), profit tier thresholds, position sizing
Chandelier22Chandelier Exit — a wider stop based on recent highest high
SuperTrend10SuperTrend-style stop — shorter, more responsive
True Range = MAX(High - Low, |High - Previous Close|, |Low - Previous Close|)
ATR = Wilder's Moving Average of True Range over N periods

Trailing stop formulas — the system calculates all of these and picks the highest (safest) value:

Stop TypeFormulaWhat it means in plain English
Chandelier Exit Highest High(22) - 3 × ATR(22) Take the highest price in the last 22 days. Subtract 3 times the average daily movement. If price drops below this, the uptrend is likely broken.
ATR Trail — Tight Close - 2 × ATR(14) Current price minus 2 day's worth of movement. Tighter = less room to breathe. Used for stocks in later stages of a move.
ATR Trail — Wide Close - 3 × ATR(14) Current price minus 3 day's worth of movement. Wider = more breathing room. Used early in a trade when you want to give it space.
SuperTrend-style Close - 2 × ATR(10) Short-period ATR — reacts faster to recent volatility changes.
Minimum guard Close - 1 × ATR(14) Floor level. The system never sets a stop tighter than 1 ATR below current price. Prevents getting stopped out by normal noise.
How the system picks It calculates ALL of the above stop levels and takes the highest value (the most protective one). So if the Chandelier Exit says ₹480, the tight trail says ₹475, and the wide trail says ₹465 — the system uses ₹480 as the stop. Stops only move UP, never down.
💡 Real-world analogy: ATR is like knowing the typical turbulence on a flight route. If you know the route is bumpy (high ATR), you tighten your seatbelt more (wider stop) and don't panic at every wobble. If the route is smooth (low ATR), a sudden jolt means something is actually wrong (tighter stop). Setting stops without considering ATR is like wearing the same seatbelt setting on every flight regardless of conditions.

Keltner Channels & T7 Squeeze Squeeze Validator

Keltner Channels Volatility
Plain English
Keltner Channels are another set of bands drawn around price — but instead of using standard deviation (like Bollinger Bands), they use ATR (Average True Range). This makes them smoother and less jumpy. On their own, KC is just a volatility envelope. But when you combine KC with Bollinger Bands, something powerful happens: you can detect genuine volatility compression — the kind that precedes the biggest breakout moves. When Bollinger Bands contract inside the wider Keltner Channels, it means statistical volatility has dropped below range-based volatility. That's a real coiled spring, not just a quiet day.
🎯 Takeaway: BBW alone tells you "bands are narrow." KC Squeeze tells you "bands are narrow relative to what's normal for this stock's range." It's the difference between a genuinely coiled spring and a stock that just happens to be quiet. The system's T7 strategy — which requires deep KC squeeze + duration + CMF confirmation — achieved 53.0% win rate, 1.72 profit factor across 1,795 trades on 2,183 NSE stocks over 15 years, versus the baseline 49.8% / 1.49.
ParameterValueWhat it controls
EMA Period20Center line — 20-day Exponential Moving Average (smoother than SMA, reacts faster to recent prices)
ATR Period14Same ATR used for stop losses — measures the stock's average daily range
ATR Multiplier1.5Width of the channels. 1.5× ATR on each side of the EMA. Wider than BB's 2σ in calm markets, narrower in volatile ones.
Min Squeeze Duration6 barsBB must sit inside KC for at least 6 consecutive days to qualify as a genuine squeeze (not noise)
Min Squeeze Intensity30%BB width must be at least 30% narrower than KC width — shallow compressions don't count
KC Upper = EMA(20) + 1.5 × ATR(14)
KC Lower = EMA(20) - 1.5 × ATR(14)

KC Squeeze = (BB Upper < KC Upper) AND (BB Lower > KC Lower)
Squeeze Intensity = 1 - (BB Width / KC Width) × 100%

The 5 squeeze states — what each means:

🎯 T7 PASS
Highest Conviction
Squeeze ≥6 bars, intensity ≥30%, CMF positive. All conditions met. +10 bonus points to entry score. This is the setup that backtested to 53% WR and 1.72 PF.
🟠 BUILDING
Squeeze Active
BB is inside KC but duration or intensity hasn't hit the T7 threshold yet. The spring is coiling — watch for the conditions to fill.
🟡 PARTIAL
Squeeze Released
A squeeze was recently active and bands have started expanding. If CMF is positive, the breakout direction is probably bullish. If negative — higher head-fake risk.
⚪ NO SQUEEZE

BB bands are wider than or equal to KC. No compression in play. This is the default state for most stocks most of the time.

T7 strategy — the three gates:

Intensity ≥ 30% BB width must be at least 30% narrower than KC width Shallow compressions (10-20%) don't store enough energy. Deep compressions (30%+) mean the stock is genuinely coiled — BB is much tighter than what ATR-based range suggests.
CMF > 0 Chaikin Money Flow must be positive (institutional buying) A squeeze without institutional support can break in either direction (or head-fake). Positive CMF confirms that big money is positioning for the upside before the release. Without CMF, max consecutive losses jumped from 8 to 10.

Keltner Channels are like a blood pressure cuff — they measure the overall range of movement (ATR). Volume Confirmation

Volume + Moving Average Volume
Plain English
Volume is simply how many shares were traded in a day. It's the most basic and most honest indicator — you can't fake volume. A price move on high volume is like a crowd cheering — lots of people agree with the move. A price move on low volume is like one person clapping — nobody cares, and the move can easily reverse. The system uses a 50-day average of volume as a benchmark — is today's volume above or below normal?
📣 Takeaway: Volume is the lie detector for price moves. A breakout above the Bollinger Band on high volume = real deal. Same breakout on low volume = suspicious, could be a fake-out. The system requires volume above the 50-day SMA for M1 entry (condition #3).
ParameterValueWhat it controls
Volume SMA Period50Average volume over ~2.5 months. Smooth enough to filter out single-day spikes.
Minimum Volume Filter200,000 shares/dayStocks below this are filtered out entirely — too thinly traded, prone to manipulation.
Volume > 50-day SMA
Above-Average Activity
More people are trading than usual. Price moves are more reliable. In M1, this is buy condition #3 (adds +20 to entry score).
Volume < 0.8 × Average
Fading Volume
Volume dropping below 80% of average = participation dying. Adds +15 exit points in Exit Intelligence. The crowd is leaving.
TradingView Setup Add the built-in "Volume" indicator. Then separately add a "Moving Average" indicator, but apply it to the volume pane (not price) — set Length = 50, MA Type = SMA. The SMA line on the volume pane tells you at a glance if today's volume is above or below normal.

Think of them like 4 different plays in a playbook: you pick the one that matches the current situation. M1 is for explosive breakouts after quiet periods. M2 is for riding strong trends. Trailing Stop Profit Tiers Risk Mgmt

System
Plain English
As your trade makes more and more profit, the system automatically ratchets up your stop loss through 4 tiers — like a safety net that rises with you. The key idea: stops only move up, never down. Once you've locked in profit at a certain level, you'll never give it back. ATR(14) is used to measure how far you've come — each "ATR" is roughly one average day's movement.
TierWhen It TriggersWhere Your Stop MovesWhat This Means For You
🔶 Tier 1
Breakeven		 Profit ≥ 1 × ATR(14) Entry price (breakeven) Risk eliminated. Even if the trade goes against you now, you lose nothing. This is the most important tier — it turns a risky trade into a free trade.
🔷 Tier 2
Comfortable		 Profit ≥ 2 × ATR(14) Entry + 1 × ATR(14) Profit locked in. You're guaranteed to walk away with at least 1 ATR of profit. The trade is now working well. Consider booking 1/3 of your position here.
🔸 Tier 3
Strong Winner		 Profit ≥ 3 × ATR(14) Entry + 2 × ATR(14) Substantial profit banked. The trade has been a solid winner. You have 2 ATR of locked profit. Book another 1/3 of your position.
🔹 Tier 4
Big Winner		 Profit ≥ 5 × ATR(14) Entry + 3 × ATR(14) Exceptional trade. This is a rare, outsized winner. 3 ATR of profit is locked. Trail the remaining 1/3 with a tight stop and let it run as far as it wants.

Scale-out guidance at each tier:

Tier 1 — at BB Upper Band
Book 1/3 of position. Take some money off the table while keeping most of the ride.
Tier 2 — at 1-sigma above
Book another 1/3. You've now banked 2/3 of the position. House money from here.
Tier 3+ — at 3-sigma
Trail the remaining 1/3 with a tight stop. Let this runner fly — these are the trades that make your year.
💡 Real-world analogy: Think of trailing stops like climbing a mountain with safety checkpoints. At each checkpoint (tier), you hammer in a piton — if you slip, you only fall back to the last piton, not all the way to the bottom. Tier 1 = base camp (breakeven). Each tier is a higher camp. The higher you climb, the more of your gains are protected.

Exit Intelligence Scoring When to Sell

System
Plain English
Knowing when to sell is harder than knowing when to buy. The Exit Intelligence system solves this by watching 7 different warning signals simultaneously. Each signal that triggers adds points to a "danger score." The higher the score, the more urgently you should consider selling. It's like a fire alarm system with 7 different smoke detectors — one going off might be a false alarm, but if 3 or 4 go off together, there's a real fire.
🚨 Takeaway: Don't try to remember all 7 signals — the system scores them automatically. Just watch the final score and act according to the tier: HOLD (do nothing), MONITOR (watch closely), TIGHTEN (tighten your stop), or EXIT (strongly consider selling).
