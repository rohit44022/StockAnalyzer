Philosophy — What Is RenTech?

Overview
Plain English
RenTech is a quantitative analysis engine inspired by Renaissance Technologies, Jim Simons' legendary hedge fund. Instead of relying on one indicator or gut feeling, it runs 6 statistical tests on the stock’s price history, generates 11 independent alpha signals, feeds them through a machine learning confidence scorer, and produces a single actionable verdict with risk-adjusted position sizing.
🎯 Core principle: No single signal is reliable enough to trade on its own. RenTech combines many weak signals into a strong ensemble — the same approach that made the Medallion Fund the most successful hedge fund in history. Each alpha source has a small edge (51-56% win rate individually); combined with ML validation and regime awareness, the system produces higher-conviction setups.
💡 How this differs from the real Medallion Fund: Simons' operation uses tick-level data across all asset classes, custom hardware, hundreds of PhDs, and sub-millisecond execution. This system captures the mathematical philosophy — ensemble of weak edges, statistical testing, regime awareness, Kelly sizing — but operates on daily Indian equity data for retail trading. Think of it as “the Medallion playbook adapted for NSE.”

Pipeline Flow

Overview

When you analyze a stock, the engine runs these 7 steps in sequence:

StepModuleWhat It Does
1Data ValidationChecks for missing columns, NaN gaps, negative prices. Requires ≥252 bars (1 year). Forward-fills small gaps.
2Statistical ProfileRuns 6 tests (Hurst, OU, VR, ADF, entropy, vol clustering) to understand the stock’s mathematical personality.
3Regime DetectionClassifies current market state: BULL, BEAR, SIDEWAYS, or HIGH_VOL. Also detects micro-regime (trend strength, vol compression, breadth, flow).
4Signal GenerationRuns 11 independent alpha signals. Each returns a score (-100 to +100), direction, and confidence. Combined into weighted ensemble.
5ML ScoringHistGradientBoosting classifier trained on 5.3M samples validates the signal. Returns ML confidence (0-100%) and edge assessment.
6Risk AssessmentKelly position sizing, ATR-based stops/targets, drawdown control, Indian market transaction costs.
7Final VerdictCombines signal + regime + risk + ML into action (STRONG_BUY / BUY / HOLD / SELL / STRONG_SELL / WATCH / HALT / AVOID) with grade (A-D).

Verdict Actions & Confidence Grades

Overview

Actions — the system produces one of 8 possible recommendations:

STRONG_BUY
Conviction >60%, STRONG_LONG direction, BULL or SIDEWAYS regime. The best setup the system can produce.
BUY
Conviction >40%, LONG direction, non-BEAR regime. Moderate bullish signal with reasonable agreement across alphas.
STRONG_SELL
Conviction >60%, STRONG_SHORT direction. Strong bearish consensus across multiple alpha signals.
SELL
Conviction >40%, SHORT direction, non-BULL regime. Moderate bearish signal.
WATCH
Signal conflicts with regime. Bullish signal in BEAR regime, or bearish signal in BULL regime. Wait for confirmation.
HOLD
No clear signal. Score or conviction too low for action. Wait for a higher-conviction setup.
HALT
Drawdown exceeds 8% safety threshold. All trading suspended regardless of signal quality.
AVOID
Risk rating is EXTREME. No trade recommended regardless of signal.

Hurst Exponent Foundation

Statistical
Plain English
The Hurst exponent measures whether a stock’s price movements have memory. Is today’s move related to yesterday’s, or is it random? This single number (0 to 1) tells you the most fundamental thing about a stock: should you use mean-reversion strategies or momentum strategies?
H = Hurst Exponent (Rescaled Range method, 252-day lookback)

H < 0.40 → Mean-reverting (prices tend to snap back)
0.40 ≤ H ≤ 0.60 → Random walk (no statistical edge)
H > 0.60 → Trending (prices continue in same direction)
H < 0.40 — Mean-Reverting
RenTech’s sweet spot. Price overshoots and reverts. Mean-reversion strategies (RSI extremes, Bollinger bounces, ConnorsRSI) have a mathematical edge. The lower the H, the stronger the rubber-band effect.
0.40–0.60 — Random Walk
No strong statistical edge in either direction. Both MR and momentum are coin-flips here. RenTech reduces position size or sits out.
H > 0.60 — Trending
Price tends to continue in the same direction. Momentum strategies (KST, Vortex, trend-following) work best. Mean-reversion is dangerous.
💡 Analogy: Think of a stock as a drunk walking down a street. H=0.5 means he walks randomly (random walk). H<0.4 means he keeps correcting himself — if he drifts left, he stumbles back right (mean-reverting). H>0.6 means once he starts going left, he keeps going left (trending). You trade differently based on which drunk you’re watching.

Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (Mean Reversion Speed) Speed Check

Statistical
Plain English
Hurst tells you whether a stock reverts. OU tells you how fast. The half-life is how many days it takes for price to revert halfway back to its mean. Too fast (<2 days) = microstructure noise. Too slow (>30 days) = capital is tied up waiting. The sweet spot is 3–20 days.
dX = θ(μ - X)dt + σdW

Half-life = ln(2) / θ
θ = mean reversion speed
μ = long-run mean price level
σ = process volatility
ParameterValueMeaning
Lookback60 daysWindow for OU parameter estimation
Min Half-Life2 daysBelow this = noise, not signal
Max Half-Life30 daysAbove this = too slow to trade actively
Sweet Spot3–20 daysFast enough to profit, slow enough to be real

Variance Ratio Test Efficiency Check

Statistical
Plain English
The Lo-MacKinlay Variance Ratio Test asks: “If the market were perfectly efficient (random walk), the variance of 5-day returns should be exactly 5× the variance of 1-day returns. Is it?” If the ratio is less than 1, returns are negatively autocorrelated (mean-reverting). If greater than 1, positively autocorrelated (trending).
VR(q) = Var(q-period returns) / (q × Var(1-period returns))

VR < 0.85 → Mean-reverting
0.85 ≤ VR ≤ 1.15 → Random walk
VR > 1.15 → Trending

Augmented Dickey-Fuller (Stationarity) Tradability Test

Statistical
Plain English
ADF tests whether the stock price is stationary — does it wander without bounds, or does it stay within a range? A stationary series is tradeable with mean-reversion strategies because you know it will come back. Most stock prices are non-stationary (they trend), but spreads and ratios between related stocks often are.
ADF Test: ΔY(t) = α + β·Y(t-1) + Σγ·ΔY(t-i) + ε

If p-value < 0.05 → Stationary (reject random walk hypothesis)
If p-value ≥ 0.05 → Non-stationary (cannot reject random walk)

Shannon Entropy Predictability

Statistical
Plain English
Entropy measures disorder in price movements. High entropy = chaotic, unpredictable, random. Low entropy = orderly, patterned, predictable. The system converts entropy into a predictability score (0–100). Higher predictability means the stock’s behaviour is more structured and alpha signals are more likely to work.
H = -Σ p(x) · log₂(p(x))
Normalized Entropy = H / log₂(n)
Predictability = (1 - Normalized Entropy) × 100

Volatility Clustering (ARCH) Vol State

Statistical
Plain English
Volatility clusters — big moves follow big moves, calm follows calm. This test measures persistence (how long vol regimes last) and ARCH ratio (strength of clustering). A stock in a HIGH_VOL state with strong persistence means the wild swings aren’t over yet. This drives position sizing — reduce exposure when vol is high and persistent.
LOW VOL
Calm period. Normal position sizing. Breakout potential is building.
MEDIUM VOL
Average volatility. Standard risk levels apply.
HIGH VOL
Elevated volatility. Reduce position size. Widen stops to avoid noise.

Alpha 8: ConnorsRSI Research

Alpha
Plain English
ConnorsRSI is a three-component composite designed specifically for mean-reversion trading. It combines RSI(3) (short-term overbought/oversold), streak RSI (how many consecutive up or down days, measured via RSI), and percentile rank of ROC (how today’s rate of change compares to the last 100 days). When all three components say “oversold,” the bounce probability is higher than any single component alone.
ConnorsRSI = (RSI(3) + Streak_RSI + Percentile_Rank_ROC) / 3

Streak = consecutive up days (+) or down days (-)
Streak_RSI = RSI(streak, 2) applied to the streak series
Percentile_Rank = % of 100-day ROC values below today’s ROC
LevelCRSI ValueSignal
Deeply Oversold<10Strong buy signal (+50 to +80 score)
Oversold10–20Moderate buy signal (+30 to +50)
Overbought80–90Moderate sell signal (-30 to -50)
Deeply Overbought>90Strong sell signal (-50 to -80)
🎯 Backtest result: 56-59% win rate, 1.70 profit factor across 29 Nifty-50 stocks over 15 years of NSE data. This significantly outperforms standard RSI(14).
Weight: 0.10 | Type: Mean Reversion

Alpha 9: KST (Know Sure Thing) Research

Alpha
Plain English
KST is a smoothed, weighted rate-of-change oscillator that synthesizes momentum across 4 timeframes. It weights longer-term momentum more heavily (the 30-day ROC has 4× the weight of the 10-day ROC), making it a trend-confirmation tool. Signal generation comes from the KST/Signal line crossover — similar to MACD but across more timeframes.
KST = SMA(ROC(10),10) + 2×SMA(ROC(15),10) + 3×SMA(ROC(20),10) + 4×SMA(ROC(30),15)
Signal = SMA(KST, 9)

Bullish crossover: KST crosses above Signal line → +40 base score
Bearish crossover: KST crosses below Signal line → -40 base score
Magnitude bonus: difference between KST and Signal × 5000
🎯 Backtest result: 55-58% win rate, 1.78 profit factor. Higher profit factor than ConnorsRSI because it catches larger moves (momentum type vs mean-reversion type).
Weight: 0.08 | Type: Momentum

Alpha 10: Chande Momentum Oscillator Research

Alpha
Plain English
CMO measures the ratio of up-moves to down-moves over a lookback period, naturally bounded between -100 and +100. Unlike RSI (which uses smoothed averages and lives mostly between 30-70), CMO’s wider range makes extreme readings more meaningful. It’s especially good at detecting overbought/oversold extremes and zero-line crossovers.
CMO = (Sum_Up - Sum_Down) / (Sum_Up + Sum_Down) × 100

CMO < -50 → Oversold (buy signal, +30 base)
CMO > +50 → Overbought (sell signal, -30 base)
Zero-line cross → Momentum shift (±15 bonus)
🎯 Backtest result: 57-59% win rate across NSE stocks. CMO’s advantage over RSI is its unsmoothed nature — it reacts faster to genuine momentum shifts while maintaining clear overbought/oversold zones.
Weight: 0.08 | Type: Mean Reversion

Alpha 11: Vortex Indicator Research

Alpha
Plain English
The Vortex Indicator measures positive (VI+) and negative (VI-) directional movement using true range. When VI+ crosses above VI-, it signals a bullish trend change. When VI- crosses above VI+, bearish. The spread between VI+ and VI- indicates trend strength — a widening spread confirms the trend is accelerating.
VM+ = |High(t) - Low(t-1)|
VM- = |Low(t) - High(t-1)|
VI+ = Sum(VM+, 14) / Sum(TR, 14)
VI- = Sum(VM-, 14) / Sum(TR, 14)

Bullish crossover: VI+ crosses above VI- → +40 base
Bearish crossover: VI- crosses above VI+ → -40 base
Spread bonus: |VI+ - VI-| × 150 (max ±30)
🎯 Backtest result: 56-58% win rate. Works best as a trend-confirmation tool alongside KST and Momentum. The crossover is most reliable after vol compression (squeeze) periods.
Weight: 0.08 | Type: Momentum / Trend

Macro Regime Detection Context

Regime
Plain English
The regime classifier determines the market’s current state using multiple evidence points: EMA alignment (20/50/200), ADX trend strength, volatility percentile, and volume trends. This is crucial because the same signal means different things in different regimes. A bullish RSI signal in a bear regime is a trap; in a bull regime, it’s an opportunity.
🟢 BULL
Price above 50 EMA & 200 EMA. Trend is up. Momentum and buy-the-dip strategies favoured. MR-type alphas get weight reduction.
🔴 BEAR
Price below 50 EMA & 200 EMA. Trend is down. Short signals and defensive positioning. Bullish signals overridden to WATCH.
🟠 SIDEWAYS
No clear trend. ADX < 25. Range-bound. Mean-reversion type alphas get weight boost. Breakout signals need extra confirmation.
🌊 HIGH_VOL
Volatility in top quartile. Elevated risk. Position sizes reduced automatically. Wider stops required. All signals treated with extra scepticism.

How regime affects alpha weights:

RegimeMR-Type AlphasMomentum-Type Alphas
BULLReduced (-20%)Boosted (+20%)
BEARNormalNormal (but bullish signals → WATCH)
SIDEWAYSBoosted (+30%)Reduced (-20%)
HIGH_VOLReduced (-20%)Normal
MR-Type vs Momentum-Type MR-Type: Mean Reversion, ConnorsRSI, CMO. Momentum-Type: Momentum, KST, Vortex. The system dynamically rebalances weights based on which strategy type fits the current regime.

ML Confidence Engine Intelligence

ML
Plain English
The ML scorer is a meta-learner that answers one question: “Given all the alpha scores, regime, and statistical profile, how likely is this signal to result in a profitable trade?” It doesn’t generate its own signals — it validates the ensemble output. Think of it as a senior trader reviewing the junior analysts’ recommendations.
🎯 Model: HistGradientBoostingClassifier (scikit-learn). Trained on 5.3 million samples across 2,884 NSE stocks. Walk-forward labelling: if the 5-day forward return exceeds round-trip transaction costs (0.20%), label = WIN. 55.8% test accuracy. Handles NaN features natively (no imputation needed).

Interpreting ML Scores Action

ML

ML Confidence (0–100%) is the model’s estimated probability that the signal leads to a profitable trade:

ML_PASS (≥60%)
ML agrees with the signal. The model sees historical patterns that support this trade. Grade may be promoted one level (B→A).
ML_NEUTRAL (40–60%)
Coin flip territory. The model has no strong opinion. Signal stands on its own merits without ML adjustment.
ML_CAUTION (<40%)
ML disagrees with the signal. Historical patterns suggest this setup often fails. Grade may be demoted one level (B→C). Consider reducing position size.
ML Edge ML Edge shows the model’s directional lean. ML_PASS when confidence ≥60% means the ML model actively supports the trade. Win probability and loss probability show the class probabilities from the model. The system also shows whether ML agreement/disagreement adjusted the confidence grade.
💡 Think of ML like a second opinion: Your 11 analysts (alpha signals) looked at the stock and gave their views. The ML model has seen 5.3 million similar situations before and says “in my experience, when the numbers look like this, it works X% of the time.” You don’t have to follow the ML, but when it strongly agrees or disagrees, that’s worth noting.

Kelly Criterion Position Sizing Sizing

Risk
Plain English
The Kelly Criterion calculates the mathematically optimal bet size given your edge and odds. Full Kelly is too aggressive for real trading (one bad streak wipes you out), so the system uses quarter-Kelly — 25% of the theoretical optimum. This sacrifices some theoretical return for much better survival.
Kelly% = (Win_Rate × Avg_Win - (1 - Win_Rate) × Avg_Loss) / Avg_Win
Position = Capital × Kelly% × 0.25 (quarter-Kelly)

Capped at: max 10% of capital in one stock, max 20 concurrent positions
ParameterValuePurpose
Kelly Fraction0.25Quarter-Kelly for conservative sizing
Max Position10%No single stock exceeds 10% of capital
Max Positions20Diversification floor
Max Sector30%Sector concentration limit
💡 Why quarter-Kelly? Full Kelly assumes you know your exact edge and odds — you don’t. In practice, overestimating your edge by even a small amount with full Kelly leads to ruin. Quarter-Kelly captures ~75% of the growth rate with dramatically lower drawdown risk. Simons reportedly used a similar fractional approach.
